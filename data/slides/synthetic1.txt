Natural Selection, Genetics, and Inheritance

Lecture 4

Slide 1 — Course Context

Topics Covered

Evolution and natural selection

Genetics as the mechanism of inheritance

DNA structure and function

Sources of genetic variation

Slide 2 — What Is Evolution?

Evolution is defined as a change in allele frequencies within a population

Evolution occurs across generations, not within individual organisms

Genetic change is measurable at the population level

Slide 3 — Core Principles of Natural Selection

Organisms compete for limited resources

Individuals vary in traits affecting survival and reproduction

Traits with a genetic basis can be passed to offspring

Differential reproductive success alters allele frequencies over time

Slide 4 — Darwin’s Unresolved Questions

How is variation maintained if selection favors certain traits?

Does inheritance blend traits together?

How do new traits arise in populations?

What is the biological mechanism of inheritance?

Slide 5 — Artificial Selection as Evidence

Humans selectively breed plants and animals

Trait frequencies can change rapidly across generations

Examples include dog breeds and pigeon varieties

Demonstrates the power of selection acting on heritable variation

Slide 6 — Rates of Evolutionary Change

Evolution can occur over vastly different timescales

Some traits evolve incrementally over millions of years

Others evolve rapidly under strong selective pressure

Example: structural adaptations in aquatic organisms

Slide 7 — Foundations of Genetics

Inheritance follows consistent, predictable patterns

Traits are not blended but transmitted discretely

Experimental breeding revealed underlying rules

Gregor Mendel (1856–1863)

Slide 8 — Mendel’s Experimental Approach

Crossed pea plants with distinct traits

Tracked inheritance across generations

Focused on traits with two observable forms

Identified particulate inheritance

Slide 9 — Genes and Alleles

A gene is a functional segment of DNA

Alleles are alternative versions of the same gene

Individuals carry two alleles per gene

Alleles may be identical or different

Slide 10 — Genotype vs Phenotype

Genotype: the genetic composition (allele pair)

Phenotype: the observable trait

Phenotype depends on allele interactions

Genetic information does not always directly map to appearance

Slide 11 — Dominance Relationships

Dominant alleles mask recessive alleles

Recessive traits appear only when both alleles are recessive

Dominance does not imply greater fitness

Not all traits follow simple dominance patterns

Slide 12 — Crossing Heterozygotes

Crossing Aa × Aa individuals

Gametes form via meiosis

Alleles segregate during gamete formation

Predictable genotype ratios result

Slide 13 — Predicting Inheritance

Tools

Event trees

Punnett squares

Outcomes

1:2:1 genotype ratio

3:1 phenotype ratio (dominant:recessive)

Slide 14 — Chromosomes and DNA

Genetic material is organized into chromosomes

Chromosomes consist of DNA and associated proteins

Humans have 46 chromosomes arranged in homologous pairs

Species differ widely in chromosome number

Slide 15 — Chromosome Theory of Inheritance

Genes are physically located on chromosomes

Chromosomes segregate during cell division

This explains Mendelian inheritance patterns

Established in the early 20th century

Slide 16 — Cell Division Overview

Mitosis: produces identical somatic cells

Meiosis: produces gametes

Meiosis reduces chromosome number by half

Ensures chromosome number stability across generations

Slide 17 — Meiosis and Genetic Diversity

Independent assortment of chromosomes

Crossing over during prophase I

Produces genetically unique gametes

Increases variation in offspring

Slide 18 — Linkage and Recombination

Genes close together may be inherited together

Recombination can separate linked genes

Recombination rate reflects physical distance

Generates novel allele combinations

Slide 19 — DNA Structure

DNA is a double-stranded helix

Sugar-phosphate backbone

Four nucleotide bases: A, T, C, G

Complementary base pairing ensures replication accuracy

Slide 20 — DNA as Genetic Information

Encodes instructions for protein synthesis

Replicates during cell division

Mutations introduce new genetic variation

Basis for evolutionary change

Slide 21 — Mitochondrial DNA

Located in mitochondria

Inherited exclusively from the mother

Does not undergo recombination

Useful for tracing maternal ancestry

Slide 22 — Applications of DNA Analysis

Estimating evolutionary divergence times

Reconstructing phylogenetic relationships

Identifying common ancestors

Forensics and ancestry testing

Slide 23 — Molecular Clocks

Based on neutral genetic mutations

Mutation rates used to estimate time since divergence

Assumes approximate constancy over time

Useful but imperfect tool

Slide 24 — Summary

Evolution is genetic change in populations

Natural selection acts on heritable variation

Mendelian genetics explains inheritance

DNA provides the molecular basis for evolution
