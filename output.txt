Financial accounting captures three primary business activities: **financing** (transactions with investors and creditors such as issuing stock or obtaining loans), **investing** (acquiring or disposing of resources like buildings, land, and equipment), and **operating** (day-to-day production, promotion, and sales). The outcomes of these activities are summarized in the **balance sheet**, **income statement**, **statement of stockholders’ equity**, and **statement of cash flows**. Together, these statements communicate a company’s financial position, results, and cash flows.

The **financial reporting process** begins with economic events that are analyzed, recorded, summarized, and reported according to **Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)**—the rules governing what, when, and how transactions are reported. The **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** oversees accounting and reporting for publicly traded firms and delegates standard‑setting authority to the **Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB)**, an independent organization. The **International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)** issues similar global standards known as *International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)*. **Auditors**, acting as independent evaluators, verify compliance with GAAP and issue one of four opinions—unqualified, unqualified with emphasis, qualified, or adverse—thereby enhancing credibility for decision makers.

A central focus is the **balance sheet**, which reports a company’s financial position at a specific date according to the **accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders’ Equity**. *Assets* are resources expected to benefit future operations, *liabilities* are obligations owed to creditors, and *stockholders’ equity* reflects owners’ residual claims. Sequential examples—from issuing stock and borrowing funds to purchasing equipment, prepaying rent, and buying supplies on account—demonstrate how each transaction upholds the balance in the accounting equation while reflecting real business activity.
